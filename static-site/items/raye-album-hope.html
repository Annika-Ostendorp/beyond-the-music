The Story

Released on 27 March 2026, THIS MUSIC MAY CONTAIN HOPE. is RAYE's second studio album: 17 songs arranged as a journey through four seasons. Beginning in autumn and ending in summer, the record follows a path from darkness towards light. What starts with loneliness, uncertainty and the search for easy answers gradually becomes a story about self-acceptance, perseverance and the possibility of a brighter future.

In an interview with ELLE, RAYE described the album as a story that reflects her own personal growth. Its opening chapter explores the temptation to find comfort in other people or temporary distractions, while learning to love and understand herself becomes an important part of the journey. After the emotional honesty of My 21st Century Blues (2023), RAYE wanted this second album to offer something more: music that could help listeners feel a little more hopeful during difficult moments.

The record also reflects the creative freedom she fought to regain earlier in her career. Rather than limiting herself to one genre or conventional song structures, RAYE brings together orchestral arrangements, jazz, soul and contemporary pop. She even previewed new material on tour before the album's release, allowing live performance to help shape the music.

Some of its most meaningful moments are shared with other musicians and family members. Hans Zimmer contributes to Click Clack Symphony., Al Green appears on Goodbye Henry., her grandfather features on Fields., and her sisters Amma and Absolutely join her on Joy. Together, these collaborations give the album an unusually personal and expansive character.

"Music is medicine. I've always said that."

- RAYE, official album statement, 2026

Why It Matters

My 21st Century Blues introduced RAYE's voice as an independent album artist. THIS MUSIC MAY CONTAIN HOPE. shows what she can create with that freedom: an ambitious, four-part musical journey that refuses to be confined to a single genre or conventional pop format.

Where her debut confronted painful experiences, this album asks what can come afterwards. Its message is not that life suddenly becomes easy, but that hope can exist alongside uncertainty.

On 22 October 2026, the album won the Mercury Prize, marking another milestone in RAYE's artistic journey.

The Sound

Big-band jazz, retro soul, blues, R&B and orchestral pop meet in arrangements that feel deliberately cinematic. Brass sections, sweeping strings and choral voices give many songs their dramatic scale, while quieter moments leave space for RAYE's storytelling and expressive vocals.

Hans Zimmer's contribution to Click Clack Symphony. brings film-score grandeur, while Al Green's appearance on Goodbye Henry. connects the record to classic soul. Elsewhere, electronic textures and jazz influences sit alongside full orchestral arrangements.

Rather than choosing one musical identity, RAYE allows these different sounds to coexist. The result reflects an artist embracing the creative possibilities of her independence.
