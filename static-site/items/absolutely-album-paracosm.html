The Story

Released on 20 February 2026, Paracosm is Absolutely's second studio album, following her 2023 debut CEREBRUM. Across 13 tracks, she invites listeners into an imaginary universe where childhood wonder meets the complicated emotions of adult life. The title refers to a detailed fictional world, often created during childhood and carried into adulthood. For Absolutely, this idea became the foundation of an album about imagination, vulnerability and the freedom to create without limits.

The journey towards Paracosm was anything but straightforward. In an interview with NYLON, Absolutely revealed that she had spent almost two years working on the record. During that time, her hard drive broke twice, forcing her to rebuild several songs from scratch. She also struggled with creative pressure, trying to imagine what a successful single should sound like instead of following her own instincts. Eventually, she realised that the way forward was to reconnect with the curiosity and playfulness that first made her fall in love with music.

Interestingly, the album's title came from an unexpected place: a fan's YouTube comment. After discovering the meaning of the word paracosm, Absolutely recognised something deeply personal in it. She began approaching her music as a world she could build freely, rather than a product that needed to meet other people's expectations. The song No Audience reflects this change in perspective, exploring what it means to create for yourself instead of performing for the approval of others.

The finished album explores different sides of her imagination and personal experiences. Goodbye Glitter reflects on letting go of superficial attachments, while I Just Don't Know You Yet brings emotional vulnerability to the foreground. The title track, Paracosm, was the final song she created for the project and draws inspiration from the magical atmosphere of childhood fantasy films. Together, these songs form a musical universe that feels both deeply personal and wonderfully surreal.

"I had to relearn how to create the way I did as a child - without rules, without fear."

— Absolutely, official album statement, 2026

Why It Matters

With CEREBRUM, Absolutely began establishing her own identity as a solo artist. Paracosm takes that identity further by placing her imagination at the centre of the entire project. Rather than adapting her creativity to conventional expectations, she builds an album around the freedom to experiment, tell personal stories and create something entirely her own.

The album also represents a meaningful moment in her artistic journey. After years of writing and collaborating with other musicians, Absolutely demonstrates how much of her creative world extends beyond individual songs. Music, visual storytelling, fashion and fantasy become different parts of one artistic vision.

At its heart, Paracosm suggests that growing up does not have to mean leaving imagination behind. Instead, rediscovering that sense of wonder can become a source of creativity, self-expression and emotional freedom.

The Sound

Paracosm blends alternative pop, electronica, jazz and R&B into a richly layered, cinematic sound. Atmospheric electronic textures, expressive vocals and unusual production choices create a feeling of entering different imagined environments. Rather than following one consistent musical formula, Absolutely allows each song to reveal another side of her artistic universe.

Her approach is particularly noticeable in the contrast between tracks. I Just Don't Know You Yet places emotional vulnerability at the centre of its piano-led arrangement, while Prototype explores a more futuristic, assertive sound with an unusual 5/8 time signature. The title track draws on the magical atmosphere of fantasy films, using delicate, almost otherworldly sounds to evoke the imagination of childhood.

Absolutely has spoken about visualising sounds as she creates music and her interest in film scores and animation. That connection between sound and imagery runs throughout Paracosm: its production does more than accompany the lyrics. It helps construct the imaginative world that gives the album its name.
